# SPEC mode — no page yet, or replacing one wholesale

Same check catalogue, inverted: every check becomes an **acceptance criterion**
instead of a grade. Run the intake first — without it there is nothing to derive.

**Refuse to emit a score in SPEC mode.** There is no page to score. A number here
would be theatre.

## The ordering rule, which is the whole point

> **A block may be DROPPED, never MOVED ahead of the question it depends on.**

Teach it as an **attention-budget allocation with two pinned endpoints**, never as a
validated psychological sequence. The endpoints — hero first, closer last — are
supported by convergent evidence from four literatures. **Blocks 2–5 are reorderable
and explicitly untested:** GoodUI's 141 tested patterns contain no section-ordering
pattern, Unbounce's 41,000-page dataset reports nothing on section order, and NN/g
has no article on section sequence. Put the swap on the experiment backlog instead of
defending it as fact.

Four of the eight dependencies, with their evidence:

- **Guarantee after price.** You cannot reverse a risk that has not been quantified; the risk *is* the money just named.
- **Anchor before the number, in the same block.** Tversky & Kahneman, *Science* 1974: medians of 25 vs 45 from arbitrary anchors of 10 vs 65, and the effect survives accuracy incentives.
- **The button repeats the headline's noun phrase, verbatim.** Field study, 956 visitors: verbatim repetition beat approximate repetition by >10 percentage points. One study, one platform — strong direction, unreplicated magnitude.
- **Effort-shrinker before price.** Fogg defines ability as simplicity, "a function of your scarcest resource at that moment". The price lands smaller against a job that already feels small.

## The 12-block minimum — what ships first

| # | Block | The one job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Header + call bar | Confirm in under two seconds this is the right business in the right town. Two clickable elements, no more. |
| 2 | Hero claim (H1) | `[Servicio] en [Ciudad] — [resultado]`. Grade 5–7 reading level. |
| 3 | Qualifying subhead | Who it is for + named neighbourhoods + what is included. |
| 4 | Trust strip | Real star average **with its count**, never rounded up. Licence, insurance. |
| 5 | Primary CTA | Reuses the H1's exact noun phrase + price signal + what happens next. |
| 6 | Hero visual | One real photo of real work. It is the LCP element. |
| 8 | The offer | What you get, plus the honesty line: "No incluye…". |
| 9 | Deep proof | Five dated, attributed reviews. Static cards — no carousel. |
| 11 | Price | Anchor → number → what is included → the fee you don't charge. |
| 12 | Guarantee | One named failure, one named remedy, one timeframe. Beside the price. |
| 16 | Closing CTA + form | Same button words. Four fields, labels above. |
| 18 | Footer NAP | Phone, address as text, hours, licence. ≤4 links. |

The full page adds: 7 stakes *(optional, most-deleted)* · 10 the plan (three steps) ·
13 work evidence *(compliance-gated)* · 14 objection FAQ · 15 service area and hours ·
17 owner's note *(optional)*.

**Note on 14:** the canonical late FAQ is arguably **wrong**, not merely untested.
MECLABS names proximity as a requirement, and GoodUI's 18 tested reassurance
placements are all CTA-adjacent, never terminal. Prefer distributing objection
answers next to each CTA, with a late FAQ only as a residual catch-all.

## How many sections — the falsifiable replacement

Ranges like "4–8 value props" are agency convention with no study behind them. Use
this instead:

> **One section per distinct offer facet or named objection you have evidence for,
> derived from the intake — not from a range.**

Every section traces to an intake answer. **A section with no mapped source gets
deleted, not shortened. A facet with no section is a gap.** A reviewer can audit that
in minutes; no range is auditable at all.

## Length, in the units the evidence supports

Budget in **screenfuls and read-percentage, never word count.** Only ~25% of readers
pass 1,600px. So every decision-critical element — the offer echo, one proof anchor,
a price token, and a CTA — sits **above 1,600px**, no matter how long the page runs
below that. Length below the line is fine *if* each added block retires a named
objection; it is not fine as undifferentiated prose.

For cold traffic the lever is **simpler language, not more words.**

## Copy first, design second — with the correction

The slogan is Zeldman's; cite it as doctrine, not evidence. What is actually
measured: Morkes & Nielsen (1997), a controlled 5-condition study with layout held
constant — concise +58%, scannable +47%, objective +27%, all three **+124%** measured
usability. That is the only study in the set where the words were the manipulated
variable.

But take Mark Boulton's correction, from the man who coined "content-out": it is not
content *then* design. What must precede design is content **structure** and message
**order**, not finished prose. The test of whether someone understands it: **in a
copy-first wireframe the COPY is real and the IMAGES are the placeholders.** Lorem
ipsum is the exact inversion.
